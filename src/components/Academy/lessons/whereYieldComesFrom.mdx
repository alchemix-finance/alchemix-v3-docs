Your deposit is held as the Mix-Yield Token, a vault built on Morpho Vaults V2 that
routes deposits into several yield sources at once. You hold one token. The DAO
selects the strategies behind it, sets their target weights, and rebalances as
markets shift.

Every strategy is sorted into one of three classes by risk, and the riskier the
class, the less of the vault it may hold.

- **Aggressive.** At most 20% of the vault, and no single Aggressive strategy
  above 20%.
- **Moderate and Aggressive together.** At most 60% of the vault, and no single
  Moderate strategy above 40%.
- **Conservative.** No limit, so at least 40% of the vault is always
  Conservative. The class is reserved for strategies that wrap and unwrap on
  contract, price off their own backing, and carry no duration risk.

Fill Aggressive to 20% and Moderate has 40% of the 60% they share left to it, so
the riskiest vault the DAO could allocate is 40% Conservative, 40% Moderate and
20% Aggressive.

The caps are checked when the DAO allocates. Because they are measured against the
size of the vault, a run of withdrawals can leave an existing allocation above its
cap until the DAO rebalances.

A cap is a ceiling, and the live vaults sit well under it. Open
any vault's Info tab and you get the current list: each strategy, its risk level, its
APR, and what share of the vault it holds. That is the composition your own
collateral is in, and the one to size against. The caps give you the worst case.

### Why the ceilings matter to a borrower

The caps are what make a high LTV safe to borrow at.

A price move cannot close an Alchemix position, because debt and collateral are
like-kind. Only a loss inside the vault itself can push a position toward
liquidation. A composition free to load up on risk would make a high LTV
dangerous. The ceilings are what prevent that.

The protocol takes a performance fee, a share of what the strategies earn, before
the rest is credited to the MYT price. The current rate is read live on the
[Fees](/user/concepts/fees) page.

The Choosing an LTV lesson works out what these same caps mean for the number you
pick.
