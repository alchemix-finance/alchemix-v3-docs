Say you borrowed 5,000 alUSD against a 10,000 USDC deposit in the Risk-adjusted Mix USDC vault on Ethereum, and have 2,000 USDC in your wallet. <Term id="healthFactor">Health Factor</Term>, one of the position stats across the top of the vault's page, sums up in one number how close your LTV is to the 90% borrowing limit.

Health Factor is 90% divided by your <Term id="ltv">LTV</Term>, and your LTV is your Debt divided by your Deposit. Owing 5,000 against a 10,000 deposit puts your LTV at 50%, so your Health Factor reads 1.8, which is 90% divided by 50%.

On many lending platforms, a Health Factor of 1 is the line where liquidation starts. Alchemix measures Health Factor against its 90% borrowing limit instead, so 1.00 means your LTV is exactly 90%, the <Stat name="maxltv" figure>MAX LTV</Stat> line on the health bar, where the vault stops new borrowing and withdrawals. A reading under 1.00 means your LTV is above 90%. At about 0.95, which is 90% divided by 95%, your LTV reaches 95%, the <Stat name="liqltv" figure>LIQ LTV</Stat> line, where a liquidation can start.

<Sub>Room for a loss</Sub>

Health Factor also tells you how large a loss inside the vault, such as a strategy losing funds, your position can take before your LTV reaches 90%. A loss lowers your Deposit while your Debt stays the same, so your LTV reaches 90% when your Deposit falls to your Debt divided by 0.9. With 5,000 owed that is about 5,556, or 56% of your 10,000.

That 56% is your LTV divided by 90%, which is the same as 1 divided by your Health Factor, here 1 divided by 1.8. The other 44% of your deposit is the loss your position can take before your LTV reaches 90%.

So a Health Factor of 2 means your deposit could lose half its value before your LTV reached 90%, and a Health Factor close to 1 means a small loss would take your LTV past 90%.

<PageBreak title="What still works between 90% and 95%" />

The vault stops your own borrowing and withdrawing at 90%, so your LTV goes above 90% only when your Deposit falls. That happens through a loss inside the vault or, on the ETH vaults, through a fall in the market price of a strategy priced at market. Between 90% and 95%, your position stays open and keeps earning, and a liquidation cannot start until your LTV reaches 95%.

In that band, the Alchemist blocks the actions that would raise your LTV and lets through repayments that lower it, with one condition on repayments in alUSD.

- **Borrowing stops.** Borrowable reads 0, since you already owe more than 90% of your deposit.
- **Withdrawing stops.** Available on the Withdraw tab reads 0, since the <Term id="alchemist">Alchemist</Term> keeps back 1.11 of deposit for each dollar you owe, which is 1 divided by 90%, and you hold less than that.
- **A USDC or MYT repayment of any size goes through.** The Alchemist runs no LTV check after a USDC or MYT repayment, so one that lowers your LTV but leaves it above 90% still goes through.
- **An alUSD repayment has to bring your LTV to 90% or under in a single repayment.** You might expect any repayment to go through, since it lowers your LTV. The Alchemist ends an alUSD repayment with the same LTV check it runs after a borrow, so one that would leave your LTV above 90% fails.

Repaying the same 100 in USDC went through. The app deposited the USDC into MYT and repaid with that MYT, and the Alchemist runs no LTV check after an MYT repayment, so <Stat name="debt">Debt</Stat> reads 6,900 even though your LTV is still above 90%.

LTV now reads 90.78%, which is 6,900 divided by 7,600. Repaying 60 more, in either token, brings your Debt to 6,840 and your LTV back to 90%, and any repayment beyond that frees room to borrow and withdraw again.

<Term id="redemption">Redemptions</Term> also keep running in the band, and each one lowers your LTV. A redemption cancels part of your Debt with the same amount of your Deposit, and since your Debt is the smaller of the two, that amount is a larger share of your Debt than of your Deposit.

While your LTV is under 95%, you can also close the whole loan from your own deposit on the Liquidate tab, which you will learn about later in this track.
