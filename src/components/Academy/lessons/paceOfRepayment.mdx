Redemptions clear debt. As Transmuter positions mature, the protocol converts
earmarked collateral into the asset needed to repay debt, then spreads that
repayment across the system in proportion to what each position owes.

The redemption rate is the share of total system debt repaid this way in a year. It
is a protocol-level parameter, and every position in the market sits on the same
one. Owe four times as much and four times as much gets repaid for you each year,
which puts both positions at the same point on the curve.

So the size of your loan has no say in how long it takes to clear. The redemption
rate decides that.

### What borrowing more does cost

Borrowing more costs you in two places, and time is neither of them. You pay when
your debt is redeemed, and again if you sell the alAssets below face value.

- **Redemption fee** – Charged on the amount redeemed, so it scales with how much
  you borrowed.
- **alAsset discount** – You pay this one only if you sell your borrowed alUSD or
  alETH below face value. Borrow 5,000 alUSD, sell it at 0.99, and that liquidity
  cost you roughly 50 up front. The dApp shows the current price before you
  confirm.

The Choosing an LTV lesson works out the exposure a high LTV creates while the
position stays open.

Redemptions set that pace for a position you leave alone. Repay part or all of the
debt by hand at any time and you free collateral sooner.

### What earmarking pays you

Redemptions run in cycles. When a cycle begins, the protocol earmarks a slice of
each open loan, sized to its share of total system debt. The earmarked collateral
stays in the vault until the claim settles, earning yield throughout. The
docs call this **temporal leverage**: the longer the gap between earmark and
settlement, the more yield that collateral collects for you.

Earmarked debt gets repaid with MYT. The repay tab offers the right asset for each
part of a loan.
