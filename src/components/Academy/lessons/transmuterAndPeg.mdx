An alAsset can trade below face value while its peg holds. Every alAsset in
circulation is backed by at least one unit of collateral inside the Alchemist, and
the Transmuter will exchange it for the underlying at exactly 1:1 after a fixed
term. alAssets are synthetic debt tokens, and that exchange mechanism holds the soft
peg.

So a discount is a price on waiting, and the shorter the wait the more the same
discount is worth:

> Annualised = (discount ÷ price) × (52 ÷ weeks)

Buying at 0.97 is a 3.09% gain over the term. Collect it in 20 weeks and that is
about 8.04% annualised.

### Two buyers, one effect

- **The saver** deposits alAssets into the Transmuter, waits out the term, and
  redeems 1:1. Waiting the full term carries no exit fee. Leaving the queue early
  carries an early transmutation fee, which exists to stop queue hopping.
- **The borrower** buys the same discounted alAssets to clear their own debt, since
  1 alAsset cancels 1 of debt regardless of what they paid for it.

Both are buying when the price is low, and that buying closes the gap. The
Transmuter makes the trade rational by guaranteeing the 1:1 exchange at the end
of a known wait.

If you want the liquidity immediately instead, external pools such as Curve and
Balancer trade alAssets at market price with no waiting and some slippage.
