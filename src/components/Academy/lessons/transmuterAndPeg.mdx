An alAsset can trade below face value while its peg holds. Every alAsset in
circulation is backed by at least one unit of collateral inside the Alchemist, the
contract that holds every position, and the Transmuter will exchange it for the
underlying at exactly 1:1 after a governance-set term. That guarantee is what holds
the soft peg on a synthetic debt token. There is one exception. If an exploit
leaves bad debt in the Alchemist, redemptions pay out pro-rata until the backing is
restored.

A discount pays you for waiting, and the shorter the wait, the more the same
discount is worth in annual terms:

> Annualized = (discount ÷ price) × (52 ÷ weeks)

Buying at 0.96 earns 4.17% over the term. Collect it in 20 weeks and that comes to
about 10.83% annualized. The Fixed Yield page quotes the same figure per position
as its projected fixed APR, worked from the live price and the live term.

### Who buys the discount

- **The saver** deposits alAssets into the Transmuter, waits out the term, and
  redeems 1:1, keeping the full discount. Leaving the queue early costs an early exit fee, which exists to keep deposits committed for the term.
- **The borrower** buys the same discounted alAssets to clear their own debt, since
  1 alAsset cancels 1 of debt regardless of what they paid for it.

Both are buying while the price is low, and that buying is what closes the gap. The
Transmuter makes the trade rational by guaranteeing the 1:1 exchange at the end of
a known wait.

If you want the liquidity right now, external pools such as Curve and Balancer
trade alAssets at market price. You get out the same day and take the market discount for it.
