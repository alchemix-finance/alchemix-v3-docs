A price move cannot close an Alchemix position. alETH is backed by ETH and alUSD by
USDC, so collateral and debt move together and the ratio between them holds. A 40%
drawdown in ETH leaves an ETH-denominated position exactly where it was.

The borrowing cap and the liquidation threshold do different jobs:

- **The borrowing cap sits at 90%.** Reach it and you stop borrowing. The position
  stays open and keeps earning.
- **The liquidation threshold sits at 95%.** Cross it and the protocol can close
  part of the position, taking only enough to restore a healthy ratio and leaving
  the rest alone.

### What can liquidate

A loss inside the Mix-Yield Token is the only thing that can. If a strategy is
exploited or reports negative returns, the backing behind every position falls. The
same debt now stands against less collateral, so your LTV rises on its own.

After a loss, the 95% threshold applies to the collateral that remains, so the highest starting LTV that survives it is:

> Survivable LTV = 95% × (1 − loss)

A 12% loss leaves 83.6%. A 20% loss leaves 76%.

### What a liquidation takes

When a position crosses 95%, the protocol first repays any earmarked debt from its
collateral, then takes only enough more collateral to bring the LTV back to 90%.
The liquidator is paid a fee out of what is left, and the rest of the position
stays open and keeps earning.

A position is liquidated in full in two cases: when its debt has reached its
collateral, or when the whole Alchemist has fallen below its minimum backing.
Collateral taken in a liquidation goes to the Transmuter, where it backs
redemptions.

The risk caps inside the Mix-Yield Token are what bound this number. At most 20% of
the vault may sit in Aggressive strategies, and at most 60% in Moderate and
Aggressive together, so the largest plausible loss is finite and you have a worst
case to size against.
