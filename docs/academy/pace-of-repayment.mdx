<PageBanner title="Lesson 1: The pace of repayment" />

An Alchemix loan repays itself. That much is in the name, and most people arrive
having read it somewhere.

The part that catches people out is the timing. Ask someone who has used a normal
loan how long an Alchemix loan takes to clear, and they will reach for the
intuition every other loan has taught them: borrow less, finish sooner.

Work through it below before reading on.

<PaceLab />

### What you just saw

Debt in Alchemix is cleared by redemptions. As <Term id="transmuter">Transmuter</Term>
positions mature, redemptions convert earmarked collateral into the asset needed
to repay debt, and that repayment is applied across the system in proportion to
each position's share of it.

The redemption rate is the share of total system debt that redemptions repay in a
year. It is a property of the protocol and the same rate applies to everyone in
the market. A position holding four times the debt has four times as much repaid
per year, which leaves both positions at the same point on the curve.

So the size of your loan does not decide how long it takes. The redemption rate
does, and you do not control it.

:::tip You are never locked in
The pace above describes what happens if you do nothing at all. You can repay
part or all of your debt by hand at any time to unlock your collateral sooner.
See [Repay Your Loan](/user/tutorials/repay-loan).
:::

### Why this matters when you size a position

If borrowing more does not cost you time, and there is no interest rate, it is
fair to ask what borrowing more does cost.

Two things, neither of them time:

- **The redemption fee**, charged on the amount redeemed. It scales with how much
  you borrowed.
- **The alAsset discount**, if you sell your borrowed alUSD or alETH below face
  value. Borrow 5,000 alUSD and sell it at 0.99, and you have paid about 50 for
  the liquidity up front. The dApp shows the current price before you confirm.

There is a third consideration, which is what a high LTV does to your exposure
while the position is open. That is the subject of lesson 3.
